      If I may ask you, how many of you believe in the soul? Modern science, so far, does not. But I do.

      I feel there is something—someone—around me and within me that talks to me. Well, at least it tries to. Many of us try to communicate with it, while some of us do not. Yet, we all have someone who is always with us—seeing, feeling, and experiencing everything we do—and therefore knows us better than anyone else.

      We could spend time discussing who listens to that inner voice more, less, often, or not at all, but that is a conversation for another day. In this piece, we are simply trying to understand whether it truly exists.

      Before we get into its anatomy and how it might exist, I feel it is important to ask: if we believe we have a soul, what exactly do we believe in?

      Do we imagine it as an invisible version of ourselves? A transparent form of our naked self? An air that surrounds us? A fire that ignites us? Or is it merely a voice that speaks to us? Some may call it a spirit. Some might think of it as a shadow. Others may believe it is life itself—that when it leaves us, the body dies.

      I personally feel that it is not just we, as human beings, who possess an invisible self, but the entire visible world, not just the living world, the whole cosmos. It is a blob of ever-moving energies. A system of invisible particles in constant flow that gives rise to emotions, judgements, feelings, the eagerness to act, and the urge to react. If I had to sum it all up in one word, I would call it consciousness. A soul is simply a trace of that consciousness. I believe that this invisible, ever-moving field of consciousness surrounding the physical world is where our individual souls come from—each one a drop from that vast, flowing ocean of energies. Still not detached from this main source, collective consciousness. 

      I feel that this whole field is not a homogeneous mix. It is unevenly distributed, and it is this unevenness that creates movement. If it were perfectly homogeneous, it would become static; it would remain unmoved. It is this heterogeneity that gives rise to the highs and lows of emotions and feelings, creating movement. It makes consciousness move, and in doing so, it keeps it alive.

      I feel this uneven mix called consciousness, is made of five elements, and surely more. I have still not been able to imagine it any further. But I am sure elements of specific nature which is as much a part of this universe, is also a part of the consciousness. What I am more certain about is that each drop of this ocean, or each soul of this collective consciousness, are different from each other, no two souls are exactly the same, no two drops of this ocean are the same. But each drop of this ocean has a percent of all its constituents just in different percentage combinations. 

      You would surely know someone with more fire than another, or someone with more earth, or more that of water.

      The fact that we are all different, yet come from the same source, makes us similar for sure, if not exactly the same, but what is also assured is it makes us unique. Yet that very uniqueness also connects us, making us part of a bigger and more wholesome whole. That ever changing wholesome consciousness keeps shifting and changing with time, each frame of its change is a unique state and never travels through the exact same phase, and those unique states are responsible for every new and unique soul that enters this living world.

      The transition that the soul takes is not physical, but that of space; it comes to the world we see from the world that we cannot, but still, it is connected through that invisible string, and that also connects us all. 

      And perhaps...

      That is what connects us all.

      Makes us all the same.

      What is the purpose of the soul?

      You, as a body, have a brain to think, nerves to carry the flow, and muscles to act. We could have existed as independent beings, but we would all have been flying in different directions, with no common belief. I feel the soul provides that common belief.

      Consciousness is not a homogeneous whole. Its uneven composition adds complexity, rhythm, and flow to the universe, while giving each one of us a unique character. We all share the same source, yet each of us carries a different composition of the constituents.

      None of us are perfect, yet all of us are working our way to reach the ultimate perfection (a wholesome awakening) Some of us are a bit grey, some are a little more than others and some are way too bright but still grey. I feel the soul has only one duty—to make us aware of who we are. It tries to help us in the process by two ways, two things on either side of Karma, first helps us in decision making, that leads us to that karma (Act) and once it's done, the second, it helps us accept, how was it, good, bad, worse or better to register the act in the right column, honestly.

    _template: header

    heading: |
      The transfer of the soul and where it all exists.

      I feel—or perhaps it is simply the lack of depth in my understanding so far—that the transfer of the soul happens in a very irregular and random manner.

      Sometimes it may transfer as it is into a new life, a new body, and sometimes it returns only in traces, traces back to the source.

      But what I strongly feel is that the soul exists not only in what is living, but also in what once lived.
    _template: header
  - level: h3
    heading: |
      If you go back in time, you realise that what is a rock today may have been a tree millions of years ago, or what is a stone today may once have been a snail. So, everything we see around us in nature, whether we call it living or non-living, is, in some way, actually living and has a soul—if not wholly, then perhaps in traces.

      That leads me to another thought.

      If a human being creates something—a lamp, a chair, a piece of art, or even a sculpture—does consciousness lend a part of itself to give life to that creation?

      By life, I do not mean something that moves, talks, or walks. I simply mean something that is alive from within.

      If it is created by a human being who himself carries a part of that same consciousness, does the creation also inherit a part of it?

      Deep down, I feel that the creator and the creation are not two different things.

      They are a part of each other. Their roles simply keep shifting.
